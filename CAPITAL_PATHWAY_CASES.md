# Capital pathway case studies

Reviewed 17 November 2026. These cases distinguish the financial commitment, delivery channel, downstream recipient and decisions the published evidence assigns to each participant. Amounts retain their original currency and scope. [Structured pathway records](src/data/capital-pathways.json), [methodology and taxonomy](CAPITAL_TRACING.md)

## A. Barbados Energy Smart Fund II

### Where capital originates

IDB lends up to **USD 30 million** from Ordinary Capital to the Government of Barbados under BA-L1043, signed on 6 February 2020. The EU Caribbean Investment Facility provides up to **EUR 13.26 million** under a separate grant agreement signed on 18 February 2021. Under its delegation agreement with the EU, IDB administers the grant and grants those resources to Barbados. Both contribute to the wider Sustainable Energy Investment Program. [IDB loan record](https://www.iadb.org/es/proyecto/BA-L1043), [loan contract, sections 2.01 and 4.01 and annex](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/2b447813fa6afa07b0012f42aae87805.pdf), [EU grant agreement, sections 1.01 and 2.01](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/e7a4ce31fd5747c13890bbce971ac77f.pdf), [IDB grant record](https://www.iadb.org/en/project/BA-G1002)

The EGFL accounts identify a segregated Phase II pool managed by EGFL. At 31 March 2025 they report a cumulative Barbados government pledge of **BBD 26.2 million** and cumulative receipts of **BBD 4.63 million**, rounded in Note 1. The financial statements identify the Government as the pool’s immediate capitalising party. The reviewed records leave the reconciliation of those transfers to EU proceeds, IDB loan proceeds and any domestic own-source contribution open. The sovereign incurs the repayment obligation on the IDB loan. [EGFL audited Phase II accounts, statement of changes in fund and note 1](https://egfl.bb/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/EGFL_AR2025.pdf), [IDB loan contract](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/2b447813fa6afa07b0012f42aae87805.pdf)

### How funds reach recipients and who decides

| Party | Documented function |
| --- | --- |
| IDB | Controls programme-level disbursements under contract. Its written non-objection is required for operating manual changes. It checks contractual conditions, including the subsidiary agreement for executing Component I. [Loan contract, sections 2.02, 3.01, 4.06 and 4.09](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/2b447813fa6afa07b0012f42aae87805.pdf) |
| Energy Division and its Project Execution Unit | The Energy Division is the executing agency and supervises EGFL’s implementation of Component I. The Project Execution Unit prepares progress reports for IDB. [Loan contract, sections 4.01 and 5.01](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/2b447813fa6afa07b0012f42aae87805.pdf) |
| EGFL | Manages the segregated pool and sub-executes Component I. The contract routes preparation grants and viable energy-efficiency and renewable-energy loans through EGFL. Eligible products include business loans, preparation grants, and trade-in rebates on qualifying air-conditioning units through selected retailers. [Loan annex, sections 2.02–2.04](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/2b447813fa6afa07b0012f42aae87805.pdf), [EGFL audited Phase II accounts](https://egfl.bb/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/EGFL_AR2025.pdf) |
| FundAccess and fund trustees | The Phase II cash-flow statement reports **BBD 464,946** disbursed to FundAccess during the year ended 31 March 2025. The trustees’ reporting role is visible in the audited statements, including authorisation of the statements for issue. [EGFL audited Phase II accounts](https://egfl.bb/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/EGFL_AR2025.pdf) |

**Open questions.** EGFL’s general renewable-energy materials describe a Smart Fund Technical Committee review and an EGFL Investment Committee approval step for loan memoranda. Phase II-specific limits, the authority for individual grants and rebates, and release signatories still require the final Phase II manual or implementing agreement. The IDB catalogue lists a 2019 draft. The audited FundAccess transfer also warrants tracing to onward allocations and borrower-level payments. [EGFL procedures](https://egfl.bb/financing/renewable-energy/), [IDB loan catalogue](https://www.iadb.org/es/proyecto/BA-L1043)

## B. Supporting Resilient Green Energy in the Caribbean (SuRGE)

### Where capital originates

The Government of Canada supplies **CAD 53 million** for the investment window and **CAD 5.5 million** for a complementary technical assistance grant to CDB. Canada describes the first amount in its December 2024 climate-finance communication. Global Affairs Canada is the lead department on the separate grant profile CA-3-P012684002, and CDB identifies the investment window as loan financing. CDB’s Grenada procurement disclosure names Canada’s Department of Foreign Affairs, Trade and Development for its SuRGE contribution. [Canada’s climate-finance communication, p. 8](https://www4.unfccc.int/sites/SubmissionsStaging/Documents/202501021212---Canada_Third%20BC_Dec%202024_EN.pdf), [Global Affairs Canada grant profile](https://w05.international.gc.ca/projectbrowser-banqueprojets/project-projet/details/p012684002), [CDB programme](https://www.caribank.org/our-work/programmes/supporting-resilient-green-energy-surge-caribbean-programme), [Grenada procurement notice](https://www.caribank.org/work-with-us/procurement/procurement-notices/battery-storage-system-project)

Canada reports signed financing arrangements with CDB, which administers the programme and channels resources to projects. CDB publishes eligibility for energy investments in ODA-eligible borrowing member countries and programme objectives for mitigation, resilience and women’s participation. The reviewed sources identify programme criteria and implementation roles. The executed agreements and allocation manuals are still needed to assign criteria-setting, individual allocation approval and disbursement authority across every window. [Canada announcement](https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2024/06/canada-hosts-54th-annual-meeting-of-board-of-governors-of-caribbean-development-bank-and-announces-development-funding-for-caribbean-region.html), [CDB eligibility and programme design](https://www.caribank.org/our-work/programmes/supporting-resilient-green-energy-surge-caribbean-programme)

### Documented downstream allocations

- **Dominica geothermal.** CDB announced its approval of a loan to the Geothermal Power Company of Dominica in December 2024 and subsequently announced financial close. CDB was lead arranger and lender of the **USD 34.8 million** package for that borrower, comprising **USD 25.4 million** from GCF via the IDB FP020 programme and **USD 9.4 million** from SuRGE. CDF’s **USD 15 million** loan is parallel financing from its own capital fund. The approval release identifies sponsor equity and an approximate **USD 68 million** total project cost. These observations describe components, a loan total, parallel capital and a project-cost estimate. [CDB approval](https://www.caribank.org/newsroom/news-and-events/cdb-approves-usd-348-million-loan-build-geothermal-energy-plant-dominica), [CDB financial close](https://www.caribank.org/newsroom/news-and-events/cdb-marks-major-milestone-financial-close-dominica-geothermal-energy-project)
- **Grenada battery storage.** CDB names Grenada Electricity Services Limited as a financing recipient and identifies SuRGE resources alongside UK and EU resources delivered through the separate SEEC programme. The procurement notice reports combined support. [CDB procurement notice](https://www.caribank.org/work-with-us/procurement/procurement-notices/battery-storage-system-project)

**Open questions.** The documented sources of the CDB loan to GPC are GCF and Canadian programme resources. Any separate CDB own-account contribution across SuRGE, internal approval committee or disbursement delegation remains unverified. The CDB annual report describes CAD 58.5 million as a line of credit, whereas the Canadian communication and CDB programme page separately identify loan finance and a grant. The dataset uses the split and flags the aggregate wording for review against the agreements. [CDB annual report, p. 46](https://www.caribank.org/sites/default/files/publication-resources/CDB%20ANNUAL%20REPORT%20%5B2024%5D%20SINGLE%20PGS%20%283%29.pdf), [Canada’s climate-finance communication, p. 8](https://www4.unfccc.int/sites/SubmissionsStaging/Documents/202501021212---Canada_Third%20BC_Dec%202024_EN.pdf), [CDB programme](https://www.caribank.org/our-work/programmes/supporting-resilient-green-energy-surge-caribbean-programme)

## C. Barbados Blue Loan and debt-for-nature conversion

### Capital, loan and risk

| Leg | Documented parties and role |
| --- | --- |
| US-dollar capital | Institutional investors subscribed to notes issued by **BB Blue Financing Company DAC**. This vehicle is a named lender in the Blue Loan agreement. **Credit Suisse International** was global lead arranger. [Conservation and Sustainability Commitments Agreement, preamble and Article I](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/a70a0ff22adb68db853e2d0804113f90.pdf), [TNC transaction case, figures 1–2](https://www.nature.org/content/dam/tnc/nature/en/documents/TNC-Barbados-Blue-Bonds_Case-Study.pdf), [Government tender disclosure](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-government-of-barbados-announces-the-final-results-of-its-offer-to-purchase-for-cash-its-notes-listed-below-301626665.html) |
| Barbados-dollar capital | **FirstCaribbean International Bank (Barbados) Limited** was the legal local-currency lender and facility agent and acted as domestic lead arranger. **Government of Barbados** borrowed both tranches. Clifford Chance advised the arrangers. TNC provided conservation advice to Barbados. [Government tender disclosure](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-government-of-barbados-announces-the-final-results-of-its-offer-to-purchase-for-cash-its-notes-listed-below-301626665.html), [contract](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/a70a0ff22adb68db853e2d0804113f90.pdf), [transaction counsel](https://www.cliffordchance.com/news/news/2022/09/clifford-chance-advises-credit-suisse-and-cibc-firstcaribbean-on.html) |
| Risk protection | **IDB** is the primary guarantor with **USD 100 million** coverage. **TNC** is the secondary guarantor with **USD 50 million** coverage. The named lenders benefit under the partial credit guarantee. Barbados remains the borrower and the contract cites its separate reimbursement and indemnity undertaking to TNC. The figures are contingent exposure limits for specified obligations. [Contract preamble](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/a70a0ff22adb68db853e2d0804113f90.pdf), [IDB disclosure](https://www.iadb.org/en/news/barbados-places-climate-financing-firmly-agenda-idb-nature-conservancy-support) |

The sovereign closed a dual-currency facility equivalent to **USD 146,518,800** on 20 September 2022. The government used the loan proceeds to repurchase external and domestic debt and cover transaction costs. The resulting savings are the downstream funding source for conservation. The agreements describe approval of qualifying debt-service deferral claims by the lenders or through the facility agent acting on their instructions. The lenders’ internal credit approvals and the original drawdown authorisation records were not established. [Government closing release](https://gisbarbados.gov.bb/blog/barbados-successfully-closes-landmark-debt-conversion/), [transaction case](https://www.nature.org/content/dam/tnc/nature/en/documents/TNC-Barbados-Blue-Bonds_Case-Study.pdf), [agreement definitions and recitals](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/a70a0ff22adb68db853e2d0804113f90.pdf)

### Conservation funding and decision rights

The Conservation Funding Agreement directs fiscal savings to the **Barbados Environmental Sustainability Fund Inc. (BESF)** and its endowment, the **Barbados Blue Conservation Trust**. TNC’s projection separates about **USD 23 million** for the conservation fund and **USD 17 million** for the endowment over the transaction term. Another **USD 10 million** is projected investment return behind the headline estimate of approximately USD 50 million in conservation funding. The funding supports marine conservation commitments, including marine spatial planning and protected areas. [Commitments agreement](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/0628bf0de462e1212afe38a8b4e953fa.pdf), [TNC case, pp. 3–5](https://www.nature.org/content/dam/tnc/nature/en/documents/TNC-Barbados-Blue-Bonds_Case-Study.pdf)

The **BESF Board**, with a majority of directors unaffiliated with the Barbados government, approves grant rules, budgets and project awards. The **Secretariat** runs daily operations. Eligible grant recipients include nongovernment, private-sector and government entities. [BESF by-laws, Articles 6.5.3 and 7.1](https://besf.bb/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/BESF-By-laws.pdf), [BESF strategic plan](https://besf.bb/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/BESF-Strategic-Plan-Approved-June-2024.pdf), [BESF overview](https://besf.bb/)

**Open questions.** The original investor allocation book, the local bank’s underlying funding mix, detailed drawdown instructions, realised conservation transfers, and grant-payment signatories are still unresolved. The documented arranger, contractual lender, guarantee beneficiary and conservation decision-maker appear separately in the dataset.
